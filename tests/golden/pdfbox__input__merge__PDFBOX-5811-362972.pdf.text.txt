| DIGEST\x20
| \x20
| Agency reasonably rejected the protester’s proposal as technically unacceptable\x20
| where the firm failed to provide in its proposal required hardware and third-party\x20
| software certifications.\x20
| DECISION\x20
| \x20
| Cybernet Systems Corporation protests the rejection of its proposal as technically\x20
| unacceptable under request for proposals (RFP) No. USCA-03-R-0045, issued by the\x20
| Administrative Office of the United States Courts for Linux operating system and\x20
| associated software (referred to as “Linux distribution,” RFP § C.4, at 4) and\x20
| technical assistance and support.  Cybernet argues that its proposal was\x20
| unreasonably rejected as technically unacceptable.\x20
| \x20
| We deny the protest.\x20
| \x20
| The RFP was issued on May 21, 2003, and contemplated the award of an\x20
| indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity contract for the base period and six 1-year\x20
| option periods.  The RFP contained requirements for hardware and third-party\x20
| software certifications.  With respect to the hardware certification, the RFP stated\x20
| that the \x20
| \x20
| Linux distribution supported by this contract will be run on hardware\x20
| procured via a separate procurement vehicle.  The Contractor must\x20
| certify that HP [Hewlett Packard] supports the Contractor’s Linux\x20
| distribution on the following [six identified] platforms . . . .\x20
text: default page 2
| The Contractor shall also support these platforms and configurations\x20
| on the offered Linux distribution. \x20
| HP-Contractor support of the Linux distribution in this context means\x20
| that the Linux distributor has made reciprocal arrangements with HP\x20
| so that the Linux distributor and HP will work together to resolve any\x20
| operating system/hardware problems.\x20
| RFP § C.4.1.3.1, at 5-6.\x20
| \x20
| With respect to third-party software certifications, the RFP stated that the \x20
| \x20
| applications developed by the Judiciary depend on several third-party\x20
| products.  The Contractor shall demonstrate that the following list of\x20
| [eight] independent software vendors have obtained certifications for,\x20
| and support their products under, the Linux distribution being offered.\x20
| RFP § C.4.1.3.2, at 6.\x20
| \x20
| The RFP instructed that an offeror “shall provide [in its proposal] the certifications\x20
| required in Section C.4.1.3.”  RFP § L.3, at 37. 1  The RFP further stated that proposals\x20
| would be evaluated for technical compliance, technical excellence, and price.  As\x20
| relevant here, under the technical compliance evaluation factor, the RFP provided\x20
| that an offeror’s proposal would be evaluated for full compliance with the RFP’s\x20
| minimum mandatory requirements; for a proposal to be considered technically\x20
| compliant, the RFP mandated that the offeror provide in its proposal all required\x20
| certifications.  RFP § M.1, at 43.\x20
| \x20
| Cybernet proposed a “[specific name omitted] Linux operating system product\x20
| (software distribution) with updates and technical support . . . meeting all of [the\x20
| agency’s] requirements.”  Cybernet Technical Proposal at 3.  Regarding the RFP’s\x20
| hardware certification, Cybernet stated that the\x20
| \x20
| Linux distribution supported by this contract will be certified by\x20
| Cybernet to run on hardware procured via a separate procurement\x20
| vehicle.  Cybernet certifies that HP . . . models [as listed in the RFP]\x20
| support the [Cybernet proposed] Linux distribution . . . . \x20
|                                                 \x20
| 1 Amendment No. 2 permitted an offeror up to 2 weeks from the proposal closing\x20
| time to submit any third-party software certifications not in its possession at the\x20
| closing time.  To the extent Cybernet believes that this amendment, for example,\x20
| gave an unfair competitive advantage to any particular competitor, there is no\x20
| evidence in the record to support Cybernet’s speculation.\x20
| Page 2  B-292600\x20
text: default page 3
| [Cybernet’s proposed Linux operating system] will be fully tested and\x20
| supported on these platforms and configurations.\x20
| A Cybernet--HP reciprocal arrangement will ensure that [Cybernet’s\x20
| proposed Linux operating system] and HP work together to resolve any\x20
| operating system/hardware problems.\x20
| Id. at 8, 17.\x20
| \x20
| Regarding the RFP’s third-party software certifications, Cybernet stated that it\x20
| \x20
| has made arrangements with an Ann Arbor local teammate,\x20
| [Company A], to support the third party middleware products called\x20
| for in the [RFP].  [Company A] is partnered with [12 specified vendors]\x20
| for enterprise web-based applications.  Furthermore, Cybernet has\x20
| direct relationships with [4 specified vendors] to support their\x20
| products on [the specific type of] platforms [proposed by Cybernet]\x20
| and to assure that [Cybernet’s proposed Linux operating system] works\x20
| smoothly with these vendors’ products.  Cybernet will leverage\x20
| [Company A’s] relationships with [5 specified vendors] to support\x20
| these products.\x20
| Id. at 9, 17.\x20
| \x20
| The agency evaluated Cybernet’s proposal as technically noncompliant,\x20
| i.e., technically unacceptable, for failing to provide the certifications required by the\x20
| RFP, as described above.  In this respect, while Cybernet self-certified and/or offered\x20
| to certify that the specified HP hardware supports its proposed Linux operating\x20
| system, Cybernet did not demonstrate in its proposal that it had made any reciprocal\x20
| arrangement with HP as required by the RFP.  Furthermore, while Cybernet offered\x20
| assurances that it and its teammate, Company A, have relationships with the\x20
| specified third-party software vendors, Cybernet did not demonstrate, as required by\x20
| the RFP, that these vendors have obtained certifications for, and support their\x20
| products under, the particular Linux operating system proposed by Cybernet.  For\x20
| these reasons, the agency rejected Cybernet’s proposal as technically unacceptable,\x20
| advising the firm that its proposal would no longer be considered in this competition.\x20
| \x20
| Cybernet challenges the evaluation and subsequent rejection of its proposal as\x20
| technically unacceptable.\x20
| \x20
| In reviewing an agency’s decision to exclude a proposal from further competition,\x20
| we review the agency’s evaluation of proposals to determine whether the evaluation\x20
| was reasonable and consistent with the terms of the solicitation.  Essex Electro\x20
| Eng’rs, Inc., B-284149, B-284149.2, Feb. 28, 2000, 2000 CPD ¶ 72 at 6.  An offeror must\x20
| submit an initial proposal that is adequately written and that establishes its merits, or\x20
| run the risk of having its proposal rejected as technically unacceptable.  An agency\x20
| Page 3  B-292600\x20
text: default page 4
| Page 4  B-292600\x20
| may exclude a proposal with significant informational deficiencies from further\x20
| consideration whether the deficiencies are attributable to omitted or merely\x20
| inadequate information addressing fundamental factors.  Generally, a proposal that\x20
| is technically unacceptable as submitted and would require major revisions to\x20
| become acceptable is not required to be further included in the competitive selection\x20
| process.  LaBarge Prods., Inc., B-287841, B-287841.2, Aug. 20, 2001, 2001 CPD ¶ 177\x20
| at 2.\x20
| \x20
| Here, in order for a proposal to be evaluated as technically compliant, the RFP\x20
| mandated that an offeror provide in its proposal the hardware and third-party\x20
| software certifications as described in the RFP.  With respect to the hardware\x20
| certification, Cybernet does not dispute that its proposal lacked the reciprocal\x20
| arrangement with HP as called for by the RFP.  As a result, and consistent with the\x20
| terms of the RFP, this omission reasonably rendered Cybernet’s proposal technically\x20
| noncompliant with the terms of the RFP.  To the extent Cybernet points to language\x20
| in its proposal where it self-certifies or “guarantees” compliance with the RFP’s\x20
| technical requirements, we note that this language basically reflects a “parroting”\x20
| back of the RFP language itself, which does not comport with the RFP’s specific\x20
| requirement for a reciprocal arrangement with HP.  With respect to the third-party\x20
| software certifications, by amendment, the agency permitted an offeror to submit\x20
| these required certifications up to 2 weeks after the proposal closing time.  Cybernet\x20
| does not dispute that while it discussed in its proposal the relationships that it and\x20
| its teammate have with specified third-party software vendors, Cybernet did not\x20
| furnish the requisite software certifications as called for by the RFP and the\x20
| amendment.  Again, consistent with the terms of the RFP, Cybernet’s failure in this\x20
| regard reasonably rendered its proposal technically noncompliant.  For these\x20
| reasons, we have no basis to object to the reasonableness of the agency’s rejection of\x20
| Cybernet’s proposal as technically unacceptable.\x20
| \x20
| Finally, for the first time in its submissions filed with our Office, Cybernet references\x20
| various websites and printouts for HP and the software vendors as evidence that its\x20
| proposed Linux operating system will comply with the requirements of the RFP. \x20
| Even assuming, arguendo, that these items support Cybernet’s position of\x20
| compliance, this information was not included within the four corners of Cybernet’s\x20
| proposal and, as a result, was not evaluated by the agency.  Because Cybernet did\x20
| not furnish in its proposal all of the information required by the RFP, Cybernet must\x20
| suffer the consequences of its failure in this regard, that being the agency’s\x20
| determination to reject its proposal as technically unacceptable.  Chek F. Tan & Co.,\x20
| B-277163, Sept. 8, 1997, 97-2 CPD ¶ 66 at 5.\x20
| \x20
| The protest is denied.\x20
