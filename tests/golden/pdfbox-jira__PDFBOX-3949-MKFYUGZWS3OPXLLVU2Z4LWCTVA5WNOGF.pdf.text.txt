| User’s Guide
| The FY 2011-12 Adopted Budget document consists of two volumes. Volume One contains\x20
| general information and an overview of the Adopted Budget for the City of Portland, as well\x20
| as the budgets for individual City bureaus and offices. Volume Two provides detailed\x20
| information about the City's funds and capital projects.
| Volume One - Bureau Budgets
| Mayor's Message A message from Mayor Sam Adams about the challenges, opportunities, and uncertainties\x20
| he and the four City Commissioners faced in preparing the FY 2011-12 budget. The\x20
| message highlights the Mayor’s budget priorities and the principles adhered to in crafting\x20
| the budget.
| Overviews City Overview
| The City Overview gives general information about the City of Portland, including its\x20
| demographics and government management systems.
| Budget Overview
| The Budget Overview presents the total City budget from a number of technical\x20
| perspectives, discusses significant changes to funds, and outlines the City’s overall budget\x20
| process. It also summarizes key budget decisions and delineates the links between those\x20
| decisions and City Council goals and strategic issues.
| Financial Overview
| The Financial Overview lays out the City’s financial planning process, fiscal structure, and\x20
| related policies. This section also includes the five-year forecast, a discussion of City debt\x20
| management, and highlights of key revenue and expenditure trends.
| Budget Notes
| The Budget Notes section lists issues which require further analysis or action, per Council’s\x20
| direction. The notes generally direct bureaus to undertake a particular assignment.
| Financial Summaries These summaries show Citywide revenues and expenses both in total and by service area, as\x20
| well as General Fund revenues, expenses, and authorized positions. Tables at the beginning\x20
| of Volume One summarize the City budget across all funds, list bureau expenses and total\x20
| City expenses by fund, and detail the City’s Capital Improvement Plan (CIP). The\x20
| Appropriation Schedule, Tax Levy computations, and Urban Renewal Tax Certifications are\x20
| also included. This section concludes with tables related to the City’s debt obligations.City of Portland, Oregon – FY 2011–12 Adopted Budget 1

| General Reserve Fund Fund Summary
| City Funds General Reserve Fund  \x20
| Fund Overview
| The General Reserve Fund was created in FY 1987-88 for the purpose of building a\x20
| reserve for the General Fund. It is Council-adopted policy to maintain a reserve\x20
| level equal to 10% of General Fund revenues, net of short-term borrowing.
| The policy defines the first 5% of reserves as an emergency reserve available to\x20
| fund major one-time, unanticipated expenditures or to offset unanticipated\x20
| revenue fluctuations that occur within a fiscal year. The second 5% of the reserve\x20
| fund is defined as a countercyclical reserve and is available to either maintain\x20
| General Fund programs at current service levels or to adjust expenditure growth to\x20
| match slower revenue growth during the first 18 to 24 months of a recession.
| City Council's five-year financial forecast allows using reserves in excess of the\x20
| required 10% level to fund one-time General Fund appropriations.
| Managing Agency Office of Management and Finance, Financial Services
| Significant Changes From Prior Year
| In FY 2010-11, the citizens of Portland approved a five year Public Safety Levy\x20
| which included funds for apparatus replacement in the Fire Bureau. During the\x20
| levy period, the existing ongoing funds dedicated to apparatus replacement in the\x20
| General Fund will be transferred to the General Reserve Fund. The replacement\x20
| funds will be stored in the General Reserve Fund until FY 2015-16, at which time\x20
| the levy will expire and the Fire Bureau will begin drawing on the reserve to\x20
| supplement its ongoing apparatus replacement funds. The apparatus reserves total\x20
| $2,395,473 for FY 2011-12.

| Fund Overview
| On November 7, 1998, the citizens of Portland authorized the sale of $53.8 million\x20

|  Changes to fire stations for female firefighter accommodations.
|  Response to some emergency facilities approaching the end of their useful lives.
| It was anticipated to take between eight and ten years to fully implement this\x20
| program. Of the $65.0 million program, $62.2 million will be used to improve fire\x20
| facilities and $2.8 million will be used to expand the Portland Communications\x20
| Center.

| Public Safety Service Area Funds Fire & Police Disabilty & Retirement Fund \x20
| Fund Overview
| Chapter 5 of the Portland City Charter establishes the Fire & Police Disability &\x20
| Retirement (FPDR) Fund for the sworn employees of Portland Fire & Rescue and\x20
| the Portland Police Bureau, their surviving spouses, and their dependent minor\x20
| children. The fund is supported primarily through a separate property tax levy\x20
| originally authorized by the voters in 1948. The levy is a rate-based levy, providing\x20
| a maximum rate of $2.80 per $1,000 of real market value.
| Managing Agency Bureau of Fire & Police Disability & Retirement
| \x20

| Net of tax anticipation notes, requirements for FY 2011-12 are increasing by $5.6\x20
| million, or 4.7%, from the FY 2010-11 Revised Budget. Higher than anticipated\x20
| ending fund balance for FY 2009-10, coupled with the expectation that FY 2010-11\x20
| budgeted contingency will fall to fund balance, result in a projected beginning fund\x20
| balance of $17.0 million for FY 2011-12. These additional resources will allow the\x20
| property tax levy to drop from $2.63 per $1,000 of assessed value for FY 2010-11 to\x20
| $2.47 for FY 2011-12. Property taxes will decrease by $3.7 million, or 3.5%, for FY\x20
| 2011-12. For similar reasons, borrowing requirements will decrease by $5.8 million,\x20
| or 17.8%, from FY 2010-11 to FY 2011-12.

| Fund Summary Police Special Revenue Fund
| Public Safety Service Area FundsPolice Special Revenue Fund  \x20
| Fund Overview
| Fund Purpose The Police Special Revenue Fund was established by City Council in May 2009. The\x20
| purpose of the fund is to account for restricted or committed law enforcement\x20
| revenues. Prior to implementation of the City's new financial system in FY 2008-09,\x20
| these revenues were held in trustee accounts outside of the City's budget. The City\x20
| now includes these revenues and their associated expenditures in the City's annual\x20
| budget.\x20
| The fund’s revenues include three types of asset forfeiture proceeds (federal, state\x20
| criminal, and state civil), which are tracked in three separate sub-funds. By law, the\x20
| Police Bureau may only spend asset forfeiture proceeds on certain functions such as\x20
| drug enforcement and education. Additional revenues include donations to the\x20

| Public Utilities Service Area FundsPublic Utilies Service Area Funds Closed -Water Growth Impact Charge Trust Fund\x20
| Fund Overview
| The Water Growth Impact Charge Trust Fund was established to collect and keep\x20
| contributions from wholesale customers to help pay for expanded facilities\x20
| required by growth in their demand for water. The fund was established so those\x20
| water districts that were experiencing growth would contribute a proportionate\x20
| share toward the next major supply increment, and establishment of this fund\x20
| ensured that those contributions would be kept separate from other bureau\x20
| revenues. In 1995, wholesale contributions were suspended with the deletion of the\x20
| growth section in the wholesale agreements.
| Ordinance #181360 authorized by City Council on October 17, 2007, directed the\x20
| Water Bureau to reimburse the wholesale customers who had paid into the Water\x20
| Growth Impact Charge Trust Fund. The reimbursement was made to the\x20
| wholesalers on November 14, 2007 from the Water Fund. The ordinance further\x20
| directed that the Water Growth Impact Fund will reimburse the Water Fund in FY\x20
| 2008-09 and that the Water Growth Impact Charge Trust Fund was closed by\x20
| ordinance in FY 2008-09 at the time of the FY 2010-11 budget adoption.

| Fund Overview
| The General Reserve Fund was created in FY 1987-88 for the purpose of building a\x20
| reserve for the General Fund. It is Council-adopted policy to maintain a reserve\x20
| level equal to 10% of General Fund revenues, net of short-term borrowing.
| The policy defines the first 5% of reserves as an emergency reserve available to\x20
| fund major one-time, unanticipated expenditures or to offset unanticipated\x20
| revenue fluctuations that occur within a fiscal year. The second 5% of the reserve\x20
| fund is defined as a countercyclical reserve and is available to either maintain\x20
| General Fund programs at current service levels or to adjust expenditure growth to\x20
| match slower revenue growth during the first 18 to 24 months of a recession.
| City Council's five-year financial forecast allows using reserves in excess of the\x20
| required 10% level to fund one-time General Fund appropriations.
