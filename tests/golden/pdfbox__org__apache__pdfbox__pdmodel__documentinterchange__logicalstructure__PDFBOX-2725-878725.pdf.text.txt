| Chapter 11 Proceedings Generally — After the commencement of a Chapter 11 bankruptcy case,\x20
| the debtor continues to operate its business as a "debtor in possession," unless it is subsequently\x20
| shown that a trustee should be appointed. Most actions to recover or enforce pre-bankruptcy\x20
| claims against the debtor are automatically stayed under Bankruptcy Code Section 362.\x20
| Bankruptcy Code Section 1121 states that the debtor has the exclusive right to file a plan\x20
| of reorganization for 120 days after the entry of the order for relief, unless the bankruptcy court\x20
| approves a longer or shorter exclusivity period. Following the expiration of this period, or if\x20
| certain other conditions are met, any party in interest, including a creditors' committee, may file\x20
| a plan of reorganization. Typically, debtors attempt to negotiate the terms of the plan of\x20
| reorganization with their creditors.\x20
| A plan of reorganization can modify the rights of any class of creditors and can be\x20
| confirmed by the bankruptcy court over the objections of one or more classes of creditors,\x20
| provided that such creditors receive under the plan at least as much as they would receive in a\x20
| Chapter 7 liquidation. If the bankruptcy court does not confirm a plan of reorganization, the\x20
| Chapter 11 case may be converted to a Chapter 7 liquidation case or it may be dismissed.\x20
| Purpose of Unsecured Creditors' Committee — To increase creditor participation in the Chapter\x20
| 11 case, Bankruptcy Code Section 1102 requires that the United States Trustee appoint a\x20
| committee of unsecured creditors (the "Committee") as soon as practical after the order for relief\x20
| has been entered. The Committee ordinarily consists of those creditors who are willing to serve\x20
| and hold the seven largest claims against the debtor which are representative of the creditor\x20
| body. According to a list filed by the debtor(s) your claim appears to be one of the twenty\x20
| largest unsecured claims against the debtor.  You may, therefore, be eligible to serve on the\x20
| Committee.  Note, however, that there must be at least three unsecured creditors willing to serve\x20
| before a Committee can be formed.\x20
| Powers and Duties of Unsecured Creditors' Committee — Members of the Committee are\x20
| fiduciaries who represent all unsecured creditors as a group without regard to the types of claims\x20
| which individual unsecured creditors hold against the debtor. Bankruptcy Code Section 1103\x20
| provides that the Committee may consult with the debtor, investigate the debtor and its business\x20
| operations, and participate in the formulation of a plan of reorganization.  The Committee may\x20
| also perform such other services in accordance with the interests of the unsecured creditors\x20
| whom it represents.\x20
| Employment of Professionals — Bankruptcy Code Section 1103 provides that the Committee\x20
| may, subject to the bankruptcy court's approval, employ one or more attorneys, accountants or\x20
| other professionals to represent or perform services for the Committee.  The employment of such\x20
| professionals must occur at a scheduled meeting of the Committee at which a majority of the\x20
| Committee members are present.  Professionals retained by the Committee may be compensated\x20
| from the assets of the bankruptcy estate pursuant to Bankruptcy Code Section 330.  Applications\x20
| for the payment of professional fees are monitored by the Office of the United States Trustee and\x20
| are subject to court approval. In addition, the Committee should carefully review all fee\x20
| applications and object to any excessive professional fees.\x20

| Other Matters — The Committee must select a chairperson and should adopt bylaws.  As a party\x20
| in interest, the Committee may be heard on most issues arising in the bankruptcy case.  Federal\x20
| Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 2002 requires that the Committee (or its authorized agent) receive\x20
| all notices concerning motions and hearings in the bankruptcy case.\x20
| Should you have any additional questions concerning the Committee or your potential\x20
| membership on the Committee please contact Jeffrey M. Sponder at the Office of the United\x20
