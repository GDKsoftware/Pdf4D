| Improving Generic Drug Review Performance\x20
| Generic Drug Budget Authority:  +5,561,000; 13 FTE\x20
| Generic Drug User Fees:  +$15,701,000; 34 FTE (First Year)\x20
| \x20
| 1.  Why is this initiative necessary?\x20
| \x20
| The number of generic drug applications submitted to FDA has been rising exponentially. \x20
| During the past six years, applications increased by 158 percent, rising from 307\x20
| applications in FY 2002 to 793 applications in FY 2006.  FDA estimates it will receive\x20
| 857 applications in FY 2008.  These statistics demonstrate the urgent need for increased\x20
| resources for the Generic Drug Review Program.  The additional budget authority and\x20
| proposed user fees in the Improving Generic Drug Review Initiative allow FDA to\x20
| respond to the growing number applications.  \x20
| \x20
| Generic drugs often cost 20 to 70 percent less than their brand-name counterparts.  The\x20
| promise of significant savings makes timely review of generic drug applications a vital\x20
| part of the Administration’s strategy to reduce healthcare costs associated with\x20
| prescription drugs.  \x20
| \x20

| and the FY 2008 levels, including the increased generic drug budget authority and\x20
| proposed user fees:\x20

| 2.  How does this initiative support important public health priorities?\x20
| \x20
| This initiative supports the public health priorities of the HHS 500-day plan for\x20
| Transforming Healthcare.  Specifically, the initiative supports the Secretary’s objective\x20
| for rapidly approving safe new drugs, continually monitoring drug safety after approval,\x20
| and proactively communicating new information to providers and patients.  Improving\x20
| generic drug review performance also supports other public health priorities:\x20
| \x20
| • The initiative advances Medicare Rx objectives by promoting the availability of\x20
| lower cost generic alternatives.\x20
text: default page 2
| • The initiative advances Medicaid Modernization by enabling FDA to approve\x20
| lower-cost generic drugs that generate savings to support a modernized Medicaid\x20
| program.\x20
| • The initiative supports Pandemic Preparedness by allowing prompt review of\x20
| generic drugs that support the HHS pandemic influenza response.\x20
| Furthermore, enhanced generic drug review ensures that American consumers have\x20
| additional choices when buying drugs, which will produce a dramatic return on\x20
| investment for the resources that FDA spends on generic drug review.  The\x20
| Congressional Budget Office estimates that generic drug use results in U.S. savings of\x20
| $10 billion per year.  One of the nation’s largest pharmacy benefit management\x20
| companies estimates that more extensive generic drug use could save an additional $20\x20
| billion per year.  As $60-70 billion in brand name drugs lose patent protection in the next\x20
| few years, Americans could substitute generic drugs for brand name drugs at a rate of 50\x20
| to 75 percent.  \x20
| \x20
| Enhanced generic drug review also directly supports the Secretary’s vision for improving\x20
| the human condition around the world.  The FDA Generic Drug Review Program plays a\x20
| significant role in the President’s Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR), by\x20
| approving new generic treatments for HIV and AIDS.  If FDA deems a generic drug safe\x20
| and effective – but the brand name equivalent is still under patent in the United States –\x20
| FDA can grant a tentative approval for the drug.  The tentative approval allows the drug\x20
| to be sold outside of the United States for HIV and AIDS treatment.\x20
| \x20
| 3.  What are the risks of not funding this initiative?\x20
| \x20
| Not proceeding with the Improving Generic Drug Review Initiative will result in the\x20
| inability to capture significant savings from generic drug use.  Generic drugs reduce the\x20
| cost of pharmaceutical care and allow increased access to health care for many\x20
| Americans.  Generic drugs have the same quality, strength, purity, and stability as brand-
| name drugs.  \x20
| \x20
| Government programs rely on the availability of generic drugs to hold down costs. \x20
| According to data from the National Association of Chain Drug Stores, in 2004 the\x20
| average price of a brand prescription was approximately $96.01, while the average price\x20
| of an available generic prescription was approximately $28.74.  That is a difference of\x20
| $67.27 per prescription.  \x20
| \x20
| Not funding this initiative limits the generic drugs available to treat diseases that are of\x20
| major concern in the United States and abroad.  The initiative also supports the\x20
| availability of specialized drugs for foreign countries (under PEPFAR), and to assure\x20
| availability of medical countermeasures to bioterrorism and natural disasters.\x20

| 4. What activities will these funds support?\x20
| \x20
| Budget Authority:  The budget authority increase in the Improving Generic Drug\x20
| Review Initiative directly supports core review functions in the Generic Drugs Program\x20
| and allows CDER to sustain performance in a number of important areas:\x20
| • managing the existing generic drug application backlog as quickly and efficiently\x20
| as possible\x20
| • increasing efficiency and improving generic drug review times by evaluating\x20
| ways to improve communication with industry and improve the quality of\x20
| applications\x20
| • assuring that generic drugs conform to manufacturing standards equal to the\x20
| standard for brand name drugs\x20
| • providing medical and scientific expertise necessary to fulfill the President’s\x20
| commitment to ensuring the quality of HIV/AIDS drugs purchased by the United\x20
| States for developing countries.\x20
| \x20
| User Fees:  The addition of fee resources under the Improving Generic Drug Review\x20
| Initiative will allow FDA’s Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER) to ramp-up\x20
| the Generic Drug program in FY 2008 and thereby begin to minimize the application\x20
| backlog.  CDER will hire staff to respond to the backlog.  CDER will also enhance a\x20
| range of operations, from establishing criteria for determining the bioequivalence of\x20
| complex drugs and drugs with non-traditional dosage forms, to improving the\x20
| information technology infrastructure that the generic drug program relies on to conduct\x20
| application review.  \x20
| \x20
| Following the first full year of implementing this initiative, FDA will achieve a 50\x20
| percent increase in approvals.  FDA currently performs an average of 40 approvals or\x20
| tentative approvals per month.  FDA will achieve the 50 percent increase after one full\x20
| year because FDA must first hire and train the new generic drug review staff before it can\x20
| achieve the additional approvals.  During the next five years, the goal is to meet the\x20
| statutory requirement for generic drug review by acting on 90 percent of original generic\x20
| drug applications and amendments to unapproved applications within 180 days.  \x20
| \x20
| The funds in this initiative will support all activities associated with the review of generic\x20
| drugs.  FDA will achieve increased outputs from FY 2007 to FY 2008 in four measures:\x20
| • First cycle approvals will increase from two percent of applications approved or\x20
| tentatively approved to five percent of applications approved or tentatively\x20
| approved.\x20
| • FDA will reduce the time it takes to approve 40 percent of first generic\x20
| applications without patent or exclusivity protection from 12 months to four\x20
| months.  FDA will also reduce the time it takes to approve 80 percent of first\x20
| generic applications without patent protection from 14 months to six months. \x20
text: default page 4
| This improvement depends on receiving the authority to formally expedite these\x20
| applications.\x20
| • The number of domestic pre-approval generic drug inspections will increase from\x20
| 110 to 155.\x20
| • The number of foreign pre-approval generic drug inspections will increase from\x20
| 42 to 87.\x20
| 5.  What results will FDA achieve?\x20
|  \x20
| Budget Authority:  With additional base resources for the Generics Drug Program,\x20
| CDER will be able to approve or tentatively approve as many as 550 generic drugs per\x20
| year.  The American public will enjoy more cost saving from generic drugs.\x20
| \x20
| User Fees:  With the additional resources generated by the proposed Generic Drug User\x20
| Fee, FDA will come closer to achieving generic drug review requirements.  The statutory\x20
| requirement for generic drug review is to act on original generic drug applications within\x20
| 180 days.  Currently, FDA estimates that performance toward this requirement with\x20
| existing resources in FY 2006 and FY 2007 is near 55-60 percent percent.  Although the\x20
| number of generic drug applications that FDA receives will continue to rise, FDA expects\x20
| that it can achieve the following performance toward meeting the statutory requirement: \x20
| \x20
| • 70 percent by the end of FY 2008\x20
| • 72 percent by the end of FY 2009\x20
| • 73 percent by the end of FY 2010\x20
| • 75 percent by the end of FY 2011\x20
| • 80 percent by the end of FY 2012\x20
| • 85 percent by the end of FY 2013\x20
| • 90 percent by the end of FY 2014.\x20
