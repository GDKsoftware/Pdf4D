| 5.0 MITIGATION AND UNAVOIDABLE IMPACTS\x20
| Ecological Impacts Summary of the Preferred Alternatives\x20
| The management measures in preferred alternative suite 4: Research Set Aside Allowing\x20
| Small Directed LCS Fishery, are not likely to have significant adverse ecological impacts to\x20
| target and non-target species.  All issues considered are either predicted to result in neutral or\x20
| positive ecological impacts.  The preferred alternative suite was designed to reduce mortality of\x20
| shark species based on the results of previous shark assessments (for a thorough description of\x20
| the most recent assessments, please refer to Chapter 3).  The preferred alternative suite was also\x20
| designed to reduce mortality of sea turtles and other protected species.  \x20
| \x20
| In alternative suite 4, the Quotas/Species Complexes measure would have positive\x20
| ecological impacts on all shark species.  Establishing the quotas and species complexes as\x20
| described in alternative suite 4 would maintain a level of fishing effort that would allow sandbar\x20
| shark stocks to rebuild and end overfishing of this stock as well as maintain the current status of\x20
| blacktip sharks which are not overfished.   Allocating the sandbar quota solely among vessels\x20
| operating within a research fishery while allowing non-sandbar LCS to be caught by vessels\x20
| operating within and outside a research fishery, was constructed to maintain proper quota levels\x20
| in order to rebuild these species based on recommendations from the most recent LCS stock\x20
| assessment.  Structuring the fishery in this way would continue to provide valuable data on these\x20
| shark stocks necessary for the effective management of these species while still allowing a\x20
| limited number of vessels to remain active in the fishery.\x20
| \x20
| In alternative suite 4, the Retention Limits measure would have positive ecological\x20
| impacts on sandbar and dusky sharks.  Only vessels participating in the research fishery would\x20
| be allowed to land sandbar sharks.  This reduction in fishing effort is anticipated to yield an 84\x20
| percent decrease in sandbar landings.  Even though discards of sandbar sharks could occur after\x20
| the sandbar quota is reached and non-sandbar LCS are still being targeted, this would be offset\x20
| by the proposed sandbar shark quota reduction of 82 percent compared to the status quo, which\x20
| would keep all landings plus discards of sandbar sharks below the recommended sandbar TAC of\x20
| 116 mt dw.  Since the vessels participating in the research fishery would be directing on sharks,\x20
| it is assumed that discards of dusky sharks would occur.  However, the number of PLL vessels\x20
| that can land sandbar sharks would be limited by the research fishery, so it is anticipated that the\x20
| PLL vessels would not set BLL gear for sharks, leading to an anticipated 72 percent decrease of\x20
| dusky shark discards compared to the status quo.\x20
| \x20
| The Retention Limits measure would have neutral ecological impacts for non-sandbar\x20
| LCS and porbeagle sharks.  Since all vessels with incidental or directed shark permits could\x20
| target non-sandbar LCS, but under a reduced quota compared to the status quo, this is anticipated\x20
| to result in a 7 percent decrease in non-sandbar LCS landings.  Retention limits of non-sandbar\x20
| LCS for incidental permit holders are larger than past retention limits.  Therefore, discards of\x20
| non-sandbar LCS are not anticipated to occur for incidental permit holders, leading to an\x20
| anticipated 72 percent decrease in non-sandbar LCS discards.  Possession and landing of\x20
| porbeagle sharks would be prohibited in commercial and recreational sectors.  This prohibition,\x20
|   5-1
text: default page 3
| coupled with reductions in the amount of effort with BLL gear would not change the numbers of\x20
| porbeagle landed, as these sharks are primarily targeted with PLL gear and the United States has\x20
| had minimal landings of this species.\x20
| \x20
| In alternative suite 4, the Time/Area Closures measure would have positive ecological\x20
| impacts on target and non-target species as well as protected species, marine mammals, and\x20
| essential fish habitat.  Maintaining the time/area closures as they have been implemented in\x20
| recent years would further the positive ecological effects that have been observed in reduction of\x20
| bycatch of prohibited, non-prohibited, and non-target HMS species.  The closure areas specific to\x20
| BLL gear have also been effective in reducing dusky and neonate and juvenile sandbar shark\x20
| interactions and, in the Caribbean, could have positive ecological impacts to EFH, mutton\x20
| snapper, red hind, and other reef-dwelling species (see Section 4.1.3).  Maintaining current\x20
| gillnet restrictions could have positive ecological impacts on endangered right whales.\x20
| \x20
| In alternative suite 4, the Reporting measure would have positive ecological impacts. \x20
| Requiring that all dealer reports are actually received by the Agency in a more timely fashion\x20
| would provide more frequent reports of shark landings in order to better assess quantities of\x20
| sharks landed and whether or not a closure or other management measures are warranted to\x20
| prevent overfishing.  This would decrease the likelihood that extensive overharvests of sharks\x20
| would occur.  In addition, increasing observer coverage to 100 percent for vessels in the research\x20
| fishery would be used to monitor landings, bycatch, and interactions with protected resources in\x20
| near “real-time.” \x20
| \x20
| In alternative suite 4, the Seasons measure would result in neutral ecological impacts. \x20
| Having one season rather than three seasons may result in most of the landings occurring early in\x20
| the year.  This should not have a negative ecological impact as most pupping occurs in the spring\x20
| or early summer.  Since all sandbar sharks and most of the non-sandbar LCS would be landed by\x20
| a limited number of vessels participating in the research fishery, NMFS would have some\x20
| information regarding when sandbar and non-sandbar LCS quotas would likely be reached.  The\x20
| Regions measure would also result in neutral ecological impacts.  Implementing one region was\x20
| chosen over maintaining three regions because, under potential reduced fishing effort, it is not\x20
| likely that maintaining regions would provide any ecological benefits for shark species, bycatch,\x20
| or protected resources.\x20
| \x20
| In the preferred alternative suite, the Recreational measures would result in positive\x20
| ecological impacts.  Requiring recreational anglers to possess species that are easy to identify\x20
| while prohibiting retention of species that are frequently misidentified with sandbar and dusky\x20
| sharks, would remove the possibility that a recreational angler might misidentify and actually\x20
| land a species that is overfished or experiencing overfishing.  This would decrease the possibility\x20
| that sandbar, dusky, and porbeagle sharks are landed, as they are sometimes mistaken for species\x20
| that are not overfished or experiencing overfishing.\x20
| \x20
| Alternative suite 4 would result in positive ecological impacts to protected resources and\x20
| EFH.  The Quotas/Species Complexes and Retention Limits measures would significantly reduce\x20
| the number of trips, thus reducing fishing effort.  These measures, in combination with other\x20
| measures such as Reporting and increasing observer coverage for the research fishery, may result\x20
|   5-2
text: default page 4
| in increased data collection on protected resources and EFH.  In addition, the reduction in usage\x20
| of BLL gear would reduce impacts to complex habitats, such as coral reefs in the Caribbean or\x20
| areas with soft corals in the Gulf of Mexico if these are areas in which sharks would be targeted.\x20
| Social and Economic Impacts Summary of the Preferred Alternatives\x20
| All management measures in preferred alternative suite 4: Research Set Aside Allowing\x20
| Small Directed LSC Fishery, are likely to have neutral or negative economic impacts on\x20
| fishermen and the associated communities.  However, NMFS believes that alternative suite 4,\x20
| strikes a balance between positive ecological impacts that must be achieved to rebuild stocks and\x20
| end overfishing while minimizing the severity of economic impacts that will occur as a result.\x20
| \x20
| In alternative suite 4, the Quotas/Species Complexes and Retention Limits measures\x20
| would have negative economic consequences for fishermen.  Based on the limited number of\x20
| boats that could fish for sandbar sharks in the research shark fishery, most current directed and\x20
| incidental permit holders would be prohibited from landing sandbar sharks.  However, directed\x20
| and incidental permit holders outside the research fishery would still be able to land non-sandbar\x20
| LCS, SCS, and pelagic shark species.\x20
| \x20
| The Time/Area Closures measure in the preferred alternative suite would have neutral to\x20
| negative economic consequences.   This measure would maintain the status quo in addition to\x20
| implementing 8 additional MPA closures in the South Atlantic.  These additional 8 MPAs would\x20
| be closed to BLL gear which could have negative economic impacts.  However, the overall\x20
| impact of these closures in comparison to other measures being preferred by this alternative,\x20
| such as reduced quotas and retention limits, is anticipated to be minor.\x20
| \x20
| In alternative suite 4, the Reporting measures would have neutral economic impacts. \x20
| Shark dealers would still be required to submit landings reports twice a month.  Additional\x20
| burden is not expected as a result of changing the pertinent date of post-marking to receipt by the\x20
| Agency.\x20
|  \x20
| The, Seasons and Regions measures in alternative suite 4, would result in negative\x20
| economic impacts to fishermen and dealers in the North Atlantic region.  Opening the seasons on\x20
| January 1, in all regions would provide benefits to vessels in the South Atlantic and Gulf of\x20
| Mexico regions as a larger variety of LCS and SCS are present there year-round.  The North\x20
| Atlantic fishermen may have to redistribute effort to another region which may not be cost\x20
| effective with reduced quotas and retention limits for sandbar and non-sandbar LCS.  \x20
| \x20
| The Recreational measures would result in negative economic impacts.  Recreational\x20
| fishermen may not be as willing to go shark fishing if the number of species that can be retained\x20
| is reduced and Charter/headboat operators may see a reduction in the amount of charters that\x20
| customers are willing to hire.  This would be especially true in areas where blacktip sharks are\x20
| more frequently encountered, as well as areas where other sandbar and dusky look-alike sharks\x20
| are frequently encountered.\x20

| 5.1 Mitigation Measures\x20
| No mitigation measures were specifically considered for the preferred alternative suite,\x20
| Alternative suite 4 and its corresponding management measures regarding Quotas/Species\x20
| Complexes, Retention Limits, Time/Area Closures, Reporting, Seasons, Regions, Recreational\x20
| Measures, and Protected Resources and EFH.  This is because the preferred alternative suite was\x20
| specifically selected to mitigate any potential adverse impacts.  As a result, mitigation was\x20
| explicitly addressed in the analyses conducted for selecting the preferred alternative suite in\x20
| other Sections of this DEIS including Chapters 4, 6, 7, 8, and 9.  NMFS would monitor the\x20
| impacts of the management measures in the preferred alternative suite and would consider other\x20
| mitigation measures in the future as necessary.\x20
| \x20
| As stated above, mitigation measures were explicitly addressed in the analyses conducted\x20
| for selecting the management measures in the preferred alternative suite.  For example, in\x20
| analyzing possible quotas and retention limits, the preferred research fishery approach was\x20
| selected because it may balance the need to end overfishing based on recent assessments, while\x20
| allowing a limited number of vessels to direct on sharks and provide scientific data on the status\x20
| of shark stocks for future management actions.  To mitigate some of these impacts, directed and\x20
| incidental permit holders outside of the research fishery would still be allowed to land non-
| sandbar LCS, SCS, and pelagic sharks.  The quotas and retention limits proposed in the preferred\x20
| alternative suite complies with the mandate to end overfishing, while still providing a reasonable\x20
| opportunity to target sharks and harvest the allocated quota.  It also provides additional\x20
| information on shark species, bycatch, protected resources, and EFH which are all necessary for\x20
| management of the fishery. \x20
| \x20
| Similarly, for time/area closures, other than implementing the 8 MPAs at the request of\x20
| the SAFMC, NMFS is maintaining the current time/area closures and has opted not to implement\x20
| additional large closures that were considered an option to reduce overall fishing mortality.  \x20
| \x20
| For dealer reporting, requiring all dealer reports to be received by the Agency within ten\x20
| days of the end of the reporting period would provide clarity and eliminate ambiguities regarding\x20
| late reporting, without imposing additional, more stringent reporting requirements that were also\x20
| considered an option in other alternative suites.  \x20
| \x20
| For seasons, the preferred measure to open on January 1 and close within 5 days notice of\x20
| any quota being 80 percent filled may balance the need to predict landings for non-research\x20
| vessels with the security of knowing what the research vessels are landing.  In addition,\x20
| implementing one region was chosen over maintaining three regions because it is not likely that\x20
| maintaining regions would provide any ecological benefits for shark species, bycatch, or\x20
| protected resources.  Finally, requiring recreational anglers to land species that are easily\x20
| identifiable is proposed to balance the need to end overfishing with the needs of the recreational\x20
| constituency.  \x20
| \x20
| In summary, while many of the actions taken in this amendment impose additional\x20
| restrictions on the shark fishery, NMFS specifically selected alternatives that minimize economic\x20
| impacts while accomplishing the mandate to end overfishing and implement a rebuilding plan for\x20
| overfished shark stocks.

| 5.2 Unavoidable Adverse Impacts\x20
| In general, there are no unavoidable adverse impacts expected as a result of the preferred\x20
| alternative suite and corresponding management measures of Time/Area Closures, Reporting,\x20
| Seasons, Regions, Recreational Measures, and Protected Resources and EFH.  NMFS would\x20
| continue to monitor the impact of the management measures in the preferred alternative suite and\x20
| would propose additional management measures, as necessary, to avoid any unanticipated\x20
| adverse impacts.\x20
| \x20
| However, there are unavoidable adverse socioeconomic impacts as a result of the\x20
| preferred alternative suite and corresponding management measures of Quotas/Species\x20
| Complexes and Retention Limits.  NMFS must administer and operate under the National\x20
| Standards of the Magnuson-Stevens Act which includes a mandate to prevent overfishing and\x20
| rebuild overfished stocks.  In trying to maintain shark stocks and meet the Magnuson-Stevens\x20
| Act mandate of ending overfishing, NMFS would significantly reduce fishing effort under the\x20
| preferred alternative suite.  This might result in directed and incidental shark permit holders and\x20
| dealers redirecting to other fisheries and/or leaving the fishing industry due to lowered quotas\x20
| and thus decreased effort and landings.  Participants in recreational shark fisheries would\x20
| experience negative economic impacts as a result of reducing the number of sharks that could be\x20
| legally landed.  Charter/Headboat operators would be most affected as a result of these measures\x20
| as they may see a reduction in the number of charters that customers are willing to hire.  In\x20
| addition, reporting burden would be increased significantly for Atlantic shark dealers as a result\x20
| of this alternative suite resulting in negative economic impacts.  While the increased reporting\x20
| burden would not impact shark dealer expenditures per se, it would result in more time spent\x20
| submitting dealer reports, which represents an opportunity cost for fishermen since that would be\x20
| time they could not spend conducting other activities related to their business.  In the analyses\x20
| for selecting the preferred alternative suite, NMFS had determined that the management\x20
| measures in alternative suite 4 are necessary in order to comply with the Magnuson-Stevens Act\x20
| mandate to end overfishing.  In addition, the preferred alternative suite has been determined to be\x20
| the most feasible alternative to rebuild shark stocks according to the most recent assessments.\x20
| \x20
| As described above, in aggregate, the preferred alternative suite and its corresponding\x20
| management measures are expected to have positive or neutral conservation benefits for shark\x20
| species, bycatch species, and protected resources.  This is because the preferred alternative suite\x20
| was specifically selected to mitigate any potential adverse impacts.  Any resulting economic or\x20
| social impacts, beyond those described above, are unavoidable.\x20

| 5.3 Irreversible and Irretrievable Commitment of Resources\x20
| The management measures in the preferred alternative suite would not result in any\x20
| irreversible and irretrievable commitment of resources.  There may be some minor ecological\x20
| impacts because the Atlantic shark fishery would still remain open, however, the Agency expects\x20
| fishing effort and bycatch levels to decrease considerably because of the reduced quotas and\x20
| retention limits being proposed.  The preferred alternative suite would increase observer\x20
| coverage levels and provide more documentation of interactions with bycatch and protected\x20
| resources.  These data would assist the Agency in developing additional management measures\x20
| in the future that may further reduce any deleterious impacts from shark fisheries on bycatch and\x20
| protected resources.\x20

| analyzing possible quotas and retention limits, the preferred research fishery approach was\x20
| selected because it may balance the need to end overfishing based on recent assessments, while\x20
| allowing a limited number of vessels to direct on sharks and provide scientific data on the status\x20
| of shark stocks for future management actions.  To mitigate some of these impacts, directed and\x20
| incidental permit holders outside of the research fishery would still be allowed to land non-
| sandbar LCS, SCS, and pelagic sharks.  The quotas and retention limits proposed in the preferred\x20
| alternative suite complies with the mandate to end overfishing, while still providing a reasonable\x20
| opportunity to target sharks and harvest the allocated quota.  It also provides additional\x20
| information on shark species, bycatch, protected resources, and EFH which are all necessary for\x20
| management of the fishery. \x20
| \x20
| Similarly, for time/area closures, other than implementing the 8 MPAs at the request of\x20
| the SAFMC, NMFS is maintaining the current time/area closures and has opted not to implement\x20
| additional large closures that were considered an option to reduce overall fishing mortality.  \x20
| \x20
| For dealer reporting, requiring all dealer reports to be received by the Agency within ten\x20
| days of the end of the reporting period would provide clarity and eliminate ambiguities regarding\x20
| late reporting, without imposing additional, more stringent reporting requirements that were also\x20
| considered an option in other alternative suites.  \x20
| \x20
| For seasons, the preferred measure to open on January 1 and close within 5 days notice of\x20
| any quota being 80 percent filled may balance the need to predict landings for non-research\x20
| vessels with the security of knowing what the research vessels are landing.  In addition,\x20
| implementing one region was chosen over maintaining three regions because it is not likely that\x20
| maintaining regions would provide any ecological benefits for shark species, bycatch, or\x20
| protected resources.  Finally, requiring recreational anglers to land species that are easily\x20
| identifiable is proposed to balance the need to end overfishing with the needs of the recreational\x20
| constituency.  \x20
| \x20
| In summary, while many of the actions taken in this amendment impose additional\x20
| restrictions on the shark fishery, NMFS specifically selected alternatives that minimize economic\x20
| impacts while accomplishing the mandate to end overfishing and implement a rebuilding plan for\x20
| overfished shark stocks.

| 5.2 Unavoidable Adverse Impacts\x20
| In general, there are no unavoidable adverse impacts expected as a result of the preferred\x20
| alternative suite and corresponding management measures of Time/Area Closures, Reporting,\x20
| Seasons, Regions, Recreational Measures, and Protected Resources and EFH.  NMFS would\x20
| continue to monitor the impact of the management measures in the preferred alternative suite and\x20
| would propose additional management measures, as necessary, to avoid any unanticipated\x20
| adverse impacts.\x20
| \x20
| However, there are unavoidable adverse socioeconomic impacts as a result of the\x20
| preferred alternative suite and corresponding management measures of Quotas/Species\x20
| Complexes and Retention Limits.  NMFS must administer and operate under the National\x20
| Standards of the Magnuson-Stevens Act which includes a mandate to prevent overfishing and\x20
| rebuild overfished stocks.  In trying to maintain shark stocks and meet the Magnuson-Stevens\x20
| Act mandate of ending overfishing, NMFS would significantly reduce fishing effort under the\x20
| preferred alternative suite.  This might result in directed and incidental shark permit holders and\x20
| dealers redirecting to other fisheries and/or leaving the fishing industry due to lowered quotas\x20
| and thus decreased effort and landings.  Participants in recreational shark fisheries would\x20
| experience negative economic impacts as a result of reducing the number of sharks that could be\x20
| legally landed.  Charter/Headboat operators would be most affected as a result of these measures\x20
| as they may see a reduction in the number of charters that customers are willing to hire.  In\x20
| addition, reporting burden would be increased significantly for Atlantic shark dealers as a result\x20
| of this alternative suite resulting in negative economic impacts.  While the increased reporting\x20
| burden would not impact shark dealer expenditures per se, it would result in more time spent\x20
| submitting dealer reports, which represents an opportunity cost for fishermen since that would be\x20
| time they could not spend conducting other activities related to their business.  In the analyses\x20
| for selecting the preferred alternative suite, NMFS had determined that the management\x20
| measures in alternative suite 4 are necessary in order to comply with the Magnuson-Stevens Act\x20
| mandate to end overfishing.  In addition, the preferred alternative suite has been determined to be\x20
| the most feasible alternative to rebuild shark stocks according to the most recent assessments.\x20
| \x20
| As described above, in aggregate, the preferred alternative suite and its corresponding\x20
| management measures are expected to have positive or neutral conservation benefits for shark\x20
| species, bycatch species, and protected resources.  This is because the preferred alternative suite\x20
| was specifically selected to mitigate any potential adverse impacts.  Any resulting economic or\x20
| social impacts, beyond those described above, are unavoidable.\x20

| designed to reduce mortality of sea turtles and other protected species.  \x20
| \x20
| In alternative suite 4, the Quotas/Species Complexes measure would have positive\x20
| ecological impacts on all shark species.  Establishing the quotas and species complexes as\x20
| described in alternative suite 4 would maintain a level of fishing effort that would allow sandbar\x20
| shark stocks to rebuild and end overfishing of this stock as well as maintain the current status of\x20
| blacktip sharks which are not overfished.   Allocating the sandbar quota solely among vessels\x20
| operating within a research fishery while allowing non-sandbar LCS to be caught by vessels\x20
| operating within and outside a research fishery, was constructed to maintain proper quota levels\x20
| in order to rebuild these species based on recommendations from the most recent LCS stock\x20
| assessment.  Structuring the fishery in this way would continue to provide valuable data on these\x20
| shark stocks necessary for the effective management of these species while still allowing a\x20
| limited number of vessels to remain active in the fishery.\x20
| \x20
| In alternative suite 4, the Retention Limits measure would have positive ecological\x20
| impacts on sandbar and dusky sharks.  Only vessels participating in the research fishery would\x20
| be allowed to land sandbar sharks.  This reduction in fishing effort is anticipated to yield an 84\x20
| percent decrease in sandbar landings.  Even though discards of sandbar sharks could occur after\x20
| the sandbar quota is reached and non-sandbar LCS are still being targeted, this would be offset\x20
| by the proposed sandbar shark quota reduction of 82 percent compared to the status quo, which\x20
| would keep all landings plus discards of sandbar sharks below the recommended sandbar TAC of\x20
| 116 mt dw.  Since the vessels participating in the research fishery would be directing on sharks,\x20
| it is assumed that discards of dusky sharks would occur.  However, the number of PLL vessels\x20
| that can land sandbar sharks would be limited by the research fishery, so it is anticipated that the\x20
| PLL vessels would not set BLL gear for sharks, leading to an anticipated 72 percent decrease of\x20
| dusky shark discards compared to the status quo.\x20
| \x20
| The Retention Limits measure would have neutral ecological impacts for non-sandbar\x20
| LCS and porbeagle sharks.  Since all vessels with incidental or directed shark permits could\x20
| target non-sandbar LCS, but under a reduced quota compared to the status quo, this is anticipated\x20
| to result in a 7 percent decrease in non-sandbar LCS landings.  Retention limits of non-sandbar\x20
| LCS for incidental permit holders are larger than past retention limits.  Therefore, discards of\x20
| non-sandbar LCS are not anticipated to occur for incidental permit holders, leading to an\x20
| anticipated 72 percent decrease in non-sandbar LCS discards.  Possession and landing of\x20
