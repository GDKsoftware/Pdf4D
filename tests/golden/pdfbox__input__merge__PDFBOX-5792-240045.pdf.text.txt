| Long Beach, California\x20
| \x20
| People and Place\x20
| Location\x20
| Long Beach, covering 50.44 square miles of land and 15.43 square miles of water,\x20
| is located just south of Los Angeles along U.S. Highway 1 near the Los Angeles Harbor.\x20
| The community is situated in Los Angeles County at 33°46’01”N, 118°11’18”W. \x20
| \x20
| Demographic Profile\x20
|  According to the 2000 U.S. Census, the population of Long Beach was 461,522, a\x20
| 7.5% increase from the 1990 U.S. Census. In 2000 the gender structure was evenly\x20
| divided with slightly more females (50.9%) than males (49.1%). The median age of the\x20
| population in 2000 was 30.8, slightly lower than the national median of 35.3 for the same\x20
| year. According to the same data, 49.2% of the population was between the ages of 22\x20
| and 54, and 29.2% were age 17 or below. For the population 18 years and over, 71.9%\x20
| had a high school education or higher, 21.2% had attained a bachelor’s degree or higher,\x20
| and 7.0% earned a graduate or professional degree. The highest level of educational\x20
| attainment was a high school diploma/equivalency for 19.9% of the population. In 2000,\x20
| 79.8% of the population lived in family households. \x20
|  The 2000 U.S. Census shows that the racial composition was predominantly\x20
| White (45.2%), followed by Black or African American (14.9%), Asian (12.0%), and\x20
| Native Hawaiian and Other Pacific Islander (1.2%). American Indian and Alaskan native\x20
| represented less than one percent at 0.8%. Overall, 20.6% classified themselves as\x20
| belonging to some other race and 5.3% of the population identified themselves with two\x20
| or more races. A total of 35.8% identified themselves as Hispanic or Latino.\x20
| Approximately 28.6% of the population was foreign-born, with 51.0% born in Mexico\x20
| and 10.3% in the Philippines.\x20
| \x20
| \x20
| History\x20
| The Chumash are one of the earliest peoples to inhabit the greater Los Angeles\x20
| metropolitan and surrounding areas. They first settled there sometime between 5000 and\x20
| 2000 B.P.1,2 They relied heavily on a maritime economy for centuries (e.g., gathering\x20
| and fishing). By 500 AD, the Tongva Indians arrived in Southern California from the\x20
| Mojave Desert. At the onset of European migration into North America it is estimated\x20
| there were 300,000 Native Americans in California. This number was drastically reduced\x20
| as European diseases killed off approximately two-thirds of the native population during\x20
| the colonial era3. Today the Gabrieleno/Tongva Tribe is headquartered within the Los\x20
| Angeles Metropolitan Area in San Gabriel. They have sought federal recognition since\x20
| 1994 and may obtain federally recognized fishing rights as their historic use of marine\x20
| resources is well known. In 1542, before the Spaniard Juan Rodriguez Cabrillo landed on\x20
| the California coast he was met by Tongva Indians on their boats. Cabrillo was the first\x20
| European to enter what would be the Los Angeles area.4,5
| \x20
| \x20
text: default page 2
| After growing as a seaside resort – subsequent to its history as a prime ranching\x20
| region throughout the 1800s – Long beach became a booming port town. Long Beach is\x20
| now California's fifth largest city. The optimum combination of strategic location,\x20
| excellent climate, shoreline beauty, and Southern California lifestyle has attracted high\x20
| levels of trade, tourism and industry. The community’s fishing history is intimately\x20
| linked to that of Los Angeles, the urban hub of the region. Significant regional networks\x20
| exist between Long Beach and other communities that satellite the metropolitan area,\x20
| such as Long Beach’s once large Japanese-American population who worked in fish\x20
| canneries on Terminal Island.\x20
| \x20
| Infrastructure\x20
| Current Economy\x20
| The City of Long Beach community development department lists Long Beach\x20
| Unified School District, Boeing, the City of Long Beach, California State University –\x20
| Long Beach, and Long Beach Memorial Medical Center as the city’s top five major\x20
| employers.6 According to the 2000 U.S. Census, 55.9% of the potential labor force was\x20
| employed and there was a 9.4% unemployment rate (calculated by dividing the\x20
| unemployed population by the labor force). Of the population over the age of 16, 38.3%\x20
| were not in the labor force. Of the employed civilian population, 21.1% worked in\x20
| “Educational, health and social services,” 14.4% worked in “Manufacturing,” 10.3%\x20
| worked in “Retail trade,” and 8.6% worked in “Arts, entertainment, recreation,\x20
| accommodation and food services.” The local, state, or federal government employed a\x20
| total of 15.9% of the employed civilian population in 2000; however less than one\x20
| percent (0.1%) were reported as working for the government in the “Agriculture, forestry,\x20
| fishing, and mining” industries. Less than one percent (0.1%) of the employed civilian\x20
| population over the age of 16 was involved in “Agriculture, forestry, fishing, and\x20
| hunting” according to the 2000 U.S. Census.\x20
|  Long Beach’s per capita income in 1999 was $19,040 according to the U.S.\x20
| Census, while the median household income was $37,270. In 1999 a total of 22.8% of the\x20
| population was below the poverty level. In 2000 there were 171,632 housing units in\x20
| Long Beach, 95.0% of which were occupied and 5.0% were vacant. Of the occupied\x20
| housing units, 41.1% were owner occupied and 59% were renter occupied. Of the vacant\x20
| housing units, 49.1% were “For rent” and 17.3% were “For sale only.”\x20
| \x20
| Governance\x20
| Long Beach is a Charter City with a Mayor-Council form of government. The\x20
| Mayor is elected at-large and there are nine Council members, each elected to represent a\x20
| district. Long Beach has an 8.25% sales and use tax rate. Los Angeles County uses a\x20
| 12.0% Transient Lodging tax rate, which earned $10,202,899 in revenue for the 2000-
| 2001 fiscal year.7 \x20
| California state law assesses commercial vessels, charter boats, and\x20
| oceanographic research vessels at 4% of their full cash value.8 Vessels registered in\x20
| California with either the Department of Motor Vehicles or the U.S. Coast Guard\x20
| (USCG) are assessed property taxes by the county tax collector where it is moored.9\x20
| Some commercial vessels are also subject to a Ballast Water Management Fee of about\x20
| $500 per voyage.10 California levies a fuel tax of $0.18 per gallon, a portion of which\x20
text: default page 3
| goes toward marine safety and education programs and boating facility administration\x20
| and development.11
| The State of California levies landing taxes that must be paid by fishermen and\x20
| fish processors involved in the retail sale of fish products. These taxes vary by species\x20
| and range between $.0013 and $.0125 per pound of fish.12 The California Department of\x20
| Agriculture also administers two commodity commissions, the California Salmon\x20
| Council and the California Sea Urchin Commission, which charge fees for marketing and\x20
| lobbying on behalf of fishermen involved in these specific fisheries.13
|  There is a California Department of Fish and Game Marine Region Filed Office\x20
| located approximately 12 miles away in Los Alamitos. The National Oceanic and\x20
| Atmospheric Administration’s (NOAA) Southwest Regional office is located in Long\x20
| Beach. Eight Coast Guard vessels are stationed at the U.S. Coast Guard Marine Safety\x20
| Office/Group Los Angeles – Long Beach located in San Pedro. Del mar and San Diego,\x20
| the nearest cities that hold Pacific Fisheries Management Council meetings, are\x20
| approximately 92 miles and 110 miles away respectively. There is a U.S. Citizenship and\x20
| Immigration Services District Office located in Los Angeles.\x20
| \x20
| Facilities\x20
| Long Beach lies south of Los Angeles and is accessible by several highways.\x20
| Long Beach Airport, situated midway between Los Angeles and Orange County, is a\x20
| general aviation airport, which also serves privately owned aircraft. A full array of\x20
| aviation support, manufacturing and business services is readily available. \x20
| The Long Beach Unified School District serves the cities of Long Beach,\x20
| Lakewood, Signal Hill, and Avalon on Catalina Island, and encompasses a total of 95\x20
| schools. The District employs more than 8000 people, making it the largest employer in\x20
| Long Beach.14 Public safety is provided by the Long Beach Police Department and Long\x20
| Beach Health and Human Services provides comprehensive health services. The City of\x20
| Long Beach Public Works Department administers gas, water, sewer, and refuse services.\x20
| Long Beach has an extensive tourism industry with a well-established infrastructure,\x20
| including accommodations, catering to a multitude of tourism activities. \x20
| The Long Beach Harbor Department, which operates under the direction of the\x20
| Board of Harbor Commissioners, is the city agency responsible for management of the\x20
| Port of Long Beach, the largest port on the West Coast. Nearly $100 billion in\x20
| international trade of commercial goods, 90% coming from East Asian trade, moves\x20
| through the Port each year. Funding for port development and maintenance is sourced\x20
| from the lease of shipping terminals to private firms; taxpayers do not fund port\x20
| operations. The Port of Long Beach, established in 1911, now encompasses over 3000\x20
| acres of land and has plans of expansion that will alleviate pressures at the nearby Port of\x20
| Terminal Island. The Long Beach Board of Harbor Commissioners has adopted a wide-
| ranging policy, the Green Port Policy, making environmental protection a top priority at\x20
| the Port of Long Beach.15 The Port’s Small Business Enterprise Program is being\x20
| inaugurated with the signing of an agreement so the Port and the Metropolitan Water\x20
| District can share small business contractor information. The MWD has operated one of\x20
| the regions most successful small business outreach programs with more than 2000\x20
| businesses participating.16

|  The Long Beach Marina accommodates recreational vessels ranging from 18 to\x20
| 90 feet. Commercial vessel up to 200 feet may also utilize the Long Beach Marina. Most\x20
| of the slips in Long Beach are constructed of wood and come with a private dock box,\x20
| water, and power. Eight launch ramps are available for a fee of $8.00 per day. Extensive\x20
| drydock facilities are also available. Both the marina slips and drydocks usually have\x20
| long waiting lists.17

| \x20
| Sportfishing\x20
| A number of charter vessels operate out of Long Beach targeting sea bass,\x20
| rockfish, and flatfish among others. Many of these charter vessels also offer seasonal\x20
| whale watching tours. In 2002, at least nine charter businesses serviced sport fishermen\x20
| and tourists in Long Beach. There are ten license agents selling sportfishing licenses in\x20
| Long Beach. In 2000 Los Angeles County residents purchased: 76,385 resident\x20
| sportfishing licenses, 59 non-resident sportfishing licenses, 164 sport salmon punch\x20
| cards, and 174 abalone report cards. In the port group consisting of Seal Beach, Long\x20
| Beach, and San Pedro a total of 54 commercial passenger fishing vessels served 148,977\x20
| anglers in 2000. These vessels reported 883,806 landings composed of more than two\x20
text: default page 5
| dozen species. Sea bass (various species), barracuda, flatfishes (unspecified), and\x20
| rockfishes (unspecified) accounted for 47.6%, 14.1%, 10.4%, and 9.2% of the landings\x20
| respectively.\x20
| \x20
| Subsistence\x20
| Local tribal and nontribal community members might be engaged in subsistence\x20
| fishing in the Long Beach area. However, specific information on subsistence fishing in\x20
| Long Beach is not discussed in detail in this Community Profile due to the lack of\x20
| available data. The California Department of Fish and Game uses the term “recreational”\x20
| to refer to fishermen that do not earn revenue from their catch but rather fish for pleasure\x20
| and/or to provide food for personal consumption. Therefore information on subsistence\x20
| fishing in California is captured, to some degree, within the above sportfishing data. \x20

| Involvement in North Pacific Fisheries\x20
| Commercial Fishing\x20
| In 2000 there were no vessels owned by Long Beach residents that participated in\x20
| North Pacific fisheries. However, eight Long Beach residents worked as crewmembers\x20
| aboard vessels involved in North Pacific fisheries in 2000. In the same year, one\x20
| community resident held a single state permit, a Commercial Fisheries Entry Commission\x20
| salmon permit. \x20
